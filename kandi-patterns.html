Kandi stitch guide

Supplies

  • Pony beads. The standard kandi bead, roughly 9 mm across and 6 mm long with a big hole. Every chart in this app is one cell per pony bead.
  • Stretch cord. Clear elastic about 0.7–1 mm thick. Thicker cord lasts longer but fills the hole sooner, so cuffs with many passes need a thinner one.
  • Scissors, plus a clip or tape to stop beads sliding off the loose end while you work.

Knots. A surgeon's knot holds best on elastic: tie a square knot, but wrap the ends around each other twice on the first half. Pull it tight, trim the ends, and tuck the knot inside a bead.

Reading charts in this app

  • Each row of a chart goes once around your wrist. Read rows left to right unless you turn on Snake in Bead It.
  • Square grid charts line beads up in straight columns. Use them for singles, ladder-stitch cuffs and fuse beads.
  • Offset rows charts shift every other row by half a bead, the way beads sit in a peyote cuff.
  • Bead It gives you one row at a time as a list like "3× Black, 2× Pink", tracks where you stopped, and totals the beads to buy.

Singles

  1. Cut about 30 cm (12 in) of cord and clip one end.
  2. String the beads in chart order. Wrap the strand around your wrist to check the size; it should slide over your hand without stretching hard.
  3. Tie it off with a surgeon's knot and tuck the knot into a bead.

The Singles category in the library has 30-bead repeats, which suit many adult wrists. Add or remove beads to fit.

Peyote cuffs

Kandi cuffs are usually made with tubular peyote stitch. Each new bead sits in the gap between two beads of the row before, which gives the offset, brick-like look.

  1. Base loop. String the beads for rows 1 and 2 together, alternating between them. Bead It lists them in order when Peyote cuff steps is on. Tie the strand into a loop loose enough to slip over your hand, and keep the long end for stitching.
  2. Next rows. Add one bead, skip the next bead on the loop, and push the cord through the bead after it. Pull snug so the new bead pops up into the gap. Repeat all the way around.
  3. Step up. At the end of a row, go through the first bead you added in that row, so you start the next row in the right place.
  4. Finish. Knot the cord around the thread between two beads, weave the tail back through a few beads, then trim.

Kandi base loops almost always have an even number of beads, two rows' worth, which is why each row ends with a step-up. Test the base loop over your hand before you start; you can't make a cuff bigger later.

Ladder-stitch cuffs

Ladder-stitch cuffs line beads up in straight columns instead of offsetting them, so their charts are a plain square grid. Lettering often reads more cleanly this way. There are a few ways to join the rows, so follow one tutorial from start to finish when you learn it.

3D, X-base, flower and star cuffs

These advanced cuffs build layers that stand out from the wrist. Each style uses its own base and charting, which differ between tutorials, so this app doesn't chart the 3D shape itself. Once you know the technique, you can still plan each layer's colors on a square grid and step through it in Bead It.

Sizing tips

  • Cuffs must pass over your hand, so size them to your hand, not your wrist.
  • Many cuff charts in the library are 24–28 beads wide. Change the width in the editor to fit you, and keep the width even for peyote.
  • Making kandi to trade? Leave a little slack; other people's hands differ from yours.
